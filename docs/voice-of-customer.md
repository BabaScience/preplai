## The Language Map: Exact Words People Use

### Words of Frustration
"black box", "chaotic terminal outputs", "endless logs", "sifting through logs", "flying blind", "can't pinpoint", "navigating without a compass", "debugging before version control", "scrambling", "late-night calls", "silent failures", "slowly bankrupting me", "development tax", "log excavation", "educated guesswork"

### Questions They Keep Asking
- "What did the agent actually do?"
- "Which agent made the mistake?"
- "Is it the retrieval, the decision-making, or the response?"
- "Was the agent struggling or advancing?"
- "Can we replicate the run?"
- "Who is accountable?"

### What They Want (But Don't Have)
"clear execution traces", "real-time visibility", "replay capability", "human-readable", "thought process at every stage", "agent's reasoning in real-time", "accountability not just observability", "visualization dashboards that display the agent's reasoning"

## Key Statistics

- **40%** of multi-agent pilots fail within 6 months of production deployment
- **40%** of sprint time spent investigating agent failures, not building features
- **3-5x** longer to debug multi-agent systems vs single-agent
- **90%** of AI agents may never leave demo stage due to the black box problem
- Demo costs of **$5-50** become **$18K-90K/month** at production scale

---

## Copy Guidelines (for landing page, marketing, docs)

Use the customer's own words. Don't say "observability" — they're already disappointed by observability tools. Say:
- "See what your agent actually did" (not "observe your agent")
- "Cinema, not logs" (not "better logging")
- "Your agent shows its work" (not "agent monitoring")
- "Human-readable, not machine-readable" (not "structured traces")
- "Accountability, not just observability" (when differentiating from LangSmith etc.)
